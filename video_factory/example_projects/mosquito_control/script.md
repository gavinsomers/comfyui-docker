A dark five-gallon bucket can become the most useful mosquito trap on your property.
It works by offering female mosquitoes an attractive place to lay their eggs.
The important part is ensuring those eggs never become biting adults.
Place a dark bucket in shade, away from patios, doors, and play areas.
Fill it halfway with water and add grass clippings or dry leaves.
Decomposing plants make the water resemble the sheltered pools mosquitoes naturally seek.
Because that water attracts breeding, it must never be left untreated.
Add a labelled BTI mosquito product, following its exact dose and replacement schedule.
BTI targets mosquito larvae in water instead of spraying every garden insect.
That distinction matters around flowers, bees, pets, and family spaces.
One bucket is rarely enough for an entire property.
Place several stations near hedges, sheds, rain barrels, and damp shaded corners.
Protect every station from children and animals while following the product instructions.
Then inspect the property weekly and empty every accidental container holding rainwater.
Saucers, tarps, gutters, toys, wheelbarrows, and tyres can all become breeding sites.
A treated trap works best when competing water sources are removed.
After heavy rain, empty unwanted water and refresh treated stations when their labels require it.
Avoid spraying open flowers while pollinators are active.
This is a routine aimed at the mosquito life cycle, not a one-time miracle.
Check local guidance, review results weekly, and adjust placement before adding more chemicals.
